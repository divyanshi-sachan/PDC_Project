# DealPilot runs live for zero dollars, barely

DealPilot can go live in November 2026 at **$0/month**, but only as a throttled demo. The workable free stack puts FastAPI, the LangGraph worker and Postgres+pgvector together on one Oracle Always Free ARM VM. It uses EDGAR, Treasury and Damodaran for data, rotates free LLM tiers (Gemini AI Studio, Groq, Cerebras, OpenRouter `:free`, local models) behind a LiteLLM router, embeds with Voyage's free tokens or local models, serves the Next.js dashboard from Cloudflare or Netlify, and traces with Langfuse Cloud Hobby. That stack handles roughly **1–5 company analyses per day**. Two of its free tiers train on your inputs, so it is only safe for public filing text. One paid step changes the picture. A cheap paid model mix (DeepSeek V4.1-Flash and Gemini Flash-Lite as producers, Claude Sonnet 5.5 as an independent verifier) costs about **$0.31 per company at list price and $0.10–0.20 with caching, off-peak and batch pricing**. A sturdier host and database add **$15–25/month**. Market prices are the expensive part: no free price source allows public display, so a licensed public demo costs about **$130–150/month** in total, while an internal or dev deployment fits in **$50–75/month**. Of the two benchmarks in the proposal, BankerToolBench is real and permissively licensed. "FinAgent Benchmark (133 questions)" does not exist under that name; the nearest real benchmark is Vals AI's Finance Agent Benchmark v2.1.

## Free LLM tiers shrank in 2026, so a router is mandatory

The free-model situation is much worse than in 2024–25. **GitHub Models was fully retired on 2026-07-30** ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/github-models/use-github-models/prototyping-with-ai-models)). Cerebras now offers a **$5 trial credit that expires after 30 days** ([Cerebras](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/support/rate-limits)). Together has no free tier and needs a $15 minimum purchase ([benchlm.ai](https://benchlm.ai/free-tier)). Google, Groq and Mistral no longer publish most free-tier numbers and show them only in the logged-in console. Groq's public page lists Developer-plan limits for `gpt-oss-120b` and `qwen3.8-27b` of **30 RPM, 1K RPD and 200K tokens/day** ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)). OpenRouter `:free` allows 20 RPM and **50 requests/day, rising to 1,000/day after a one-time $10 credit purchase** ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits)). Cloudflare Workers AI gives **10,000 neurons/day** free ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). NVIDIA's build.nvidia.com runs at about 40 RPM according to forum threads, but no official NVIDIA page confirms that ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/request-for-nvidia-build-api-rate-limit-increase-40-rpm-200-rpm/378450)).

Gemini AI Studio is the largest free pool, and data use is its catch. Free-tier models include Gemini 4 Hydrogen, 3.8 Flash and 3.1/3.5 Flash-Lite. **Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon have no free tier**, and every free model is marked "used to improve products" ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Google's terms say human reviewers may read free-tier inputs and outputs, and tell users not to submit confidential information; EEA/UK/Swiss users are the exception and get paid-tier data terms ([Gemini API terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). The rule for DealPilot follows from this: public 10-K text can go to training tiers, but client theses, deal inputs and draft memos should go only to paid no-training APIs or local models.

Throughput is the binding constraint. One DealPilot run is about 45–60 LLM calls and ~350K input tokens (estimate below). Groq's 200K tokens/day per model therefore covers less than one run per model, and OpenRouter at 1,000 RPD covers about a dozen. **LiteLLM Router** handles that rotation best. It supports per-deployment RPM/TPM and daily RPD/TPD limits, cooldown on 429s, ordered fallbacks and exponential backoff ([LiteLLM docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/routing)). DealPilot should define one model group per agent role, rotate free deployments inside each group, and end every group with a cheap paid model.

Paid prices are now very low. Per million tokens (input/output): **DeepSeek V4.1-Flash costs $0.30/$1.20 at peak and $0.15/$0.60 off-peak**, with cache hits at $0.003–0.006. Peak hours are 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC on weekdays ([DeepSeek](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)). **Claude Haiku 5.5 is $0.10/$0.50 for prompts up to 100K tokens, and five times that above 100K**. Sonnet 5.5 is $2/$10 and Opus 5.5 is $4/$20 ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). OpenAI's gpt-6-luna is $0.10/$0.50 and gpt-6.1-sol is $2/$10 ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite is $0.25/$1.50. Budget on the announced increases: **Gemini 3.8/3.6 Flash doubles to $1.50/$7.50 on 2027-01-01, and Gemini 4 Carbon/Argon doubles to $4/$20 on 2026-12-01** ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).

The role mapping follows two principles. First, valuation arithmetic is already deterministic Python, so the modeling agent needs reliable schema-strict tool calls, not raw reasoning power. Second, the verifier and skeptic must come from a different model family than the producers, so their errors are not correlated.

| Agent role | Zero-cost choice | Low-cost paid choice | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orchestrator/planner | Local gpt-oss-20b or Gemini Flash (free) | gpt-6-luna or DeepSeek-flash | Short calls, needs dependable tool calling |
| Research (RAG over 10-K) | Gemini 3.1/3.5 Flash-Lite free (public text only); fall back to Cerebras/Groq qwen3.8-27b | Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite; Haiku 5.5 for chunks under 100K | Largest token volume, so cost per token matters most |
| Modeling (DCF/comps tool loop) | Groq gpt-oss-120b or qwen3.8-27b; local gpt-oss-20b Q8 | DeepSeek-flash or gpt-6.1-terra | Picks tools and arguments; the math runs in Python |
| Forensic | Gemini 3.8 Flash free or NVIDIA-hosted open models | DeepSeek-v4-pro or Gemini 3.8 Flash | Needs careful reading of notes and accruals |
| Skeptic | Different family from the producer | Claude Sonnet 5.5 | Independent challenge |
| Verifier | Gemini 4 Hydrogen free if producers are Qwen/gpt-oss; gpt-oss-120b if producers are Gemini | Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Opus 5.5 for high stakes; GPT-6.1-sol if Claude produces) | Highest-leverage spend in the pipeline |
| Deliverable writer | Gemini 3.8 Flash free | Claude Sonnet 5.5 or Gemini 3.8 Flash | Prose quality is visible to users |

This mapping is a synthesis of the price and limit data above. **No head-to-head accuracy data for these 2026 models on financial extraction was found**, and the BFCL tool-calling leaderboard did not render ([BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)). DealPilot's own eval suite should settle the final role assignments.

Local models cover the confidential path. On a 32 GB Apple Silicon Mac, Qwen 3.6-35B-A3B at Q4 (about 20 GB, 35–55 tok/s) is the common 2026 default ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ollama-models)). One guide prefers gpt-oss-20b at Q8 for agent loops because its JSON output is cleaner ([openclawdc](https://www.openclawdc.com/blog/best-local-llms-32gb-ram/)). Q2/Q3 quantization pushes tool calling below usable levels ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/best-ollama-models-tool-calling)). The Oracle free VM is CPU-only ARM, so by my estimate it is too slow for interactive agent loops but fine for overnight batch extraction with 4–8B models.

## Search, embeddings and reranking fit inside recurring free quotas

The web-research agents have several *recurring* free quotas. **Tavily gives 1,000 credits/month with no card, then $0.008/credit** ([Tavily](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-credits.md)). **Exa resets to $10 of credit each month** (about 2,500 Lite searches at $4/1k) and charges $1 per 1k pages for contents ([Exa](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/pricing.md)). Exa's neural "similar companies" search suits peer discovery. Brave reportedly replaced its 2,000-query free tier with a $5 monthly credit that needs a card and has uncapped overage ([apiserpent](https://apiserpent.com/blog/free-brave-search-api)). Gemini 2.5 Flash grounding allows 500 free requests/day, but **Gemini 3+ grounding is not on the free tier** ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Do not build on the **Google Custom Search JSON API: it is closed to new customers and shuts down on 1 January 2027** ([Brave](https://brave.com/learn/google-api-shutdown/)). The `ddgs` DuckDuckGo package costs nothing but is an unofficial scraper that returns 202 rate-limit responses unless held to about 1 request/second with backoff ([Agno thread](https://community.agno.com/t/duckduckgo-search-rate-limit-issue/1021)). Use it only as the last fallback. Anthropic's native web search costs $10 per 1,000 searches plus tokens ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/07/anthropic-rolls-out-an-api-for-ai-powered-web-search/)), which is too expensive as the default. For page extraction, Firecrawl Free gives 1,000 credits/month ([Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing)) and Jina Reader works without a key at about 20 RPM ([activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/jina-ai-api-pricing-token-costs-limits-2026)). Self-hosted trafilatura, with crawl4ai for JS-heavy pages, avoids quotas entirely. Filings themselves should always come from EDGAR, not scrapers.

Embeddings are close to free. **Voyage includes 200M free tokens on the voyage-4 family and 50M on voyage-finance-2**. After that, voyage-4-lite costs $0.02/M ([Voyage](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/pricing)). At roughly 100K tokens per 10-K, that covers the initial embedding of about 2,000 filings. Gemini embeddings are free but carry the same training clause. Cohere's trial key is capped at 1,000 calls/month across all endpoints and **bars production use** ([codenote](https://codenote.net/en/posts/cohere-trial-api-key-pricing-and-limits/)). Good local options are Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (1024-d, about 70.5 on MTEB-Eng v2) and EmbeddingGemma-300M (768-d, about 69.7, roughly 0.5 GB RAM) ([fourweekmba](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-google-paper-embeddinggemma-2-trails-qwen-on-text-not-code/); [surrealdb](https://surrealdb.com/blog/embedding-models-comparison)). FinMTEB found that finance-adapted embeddings "significantly outperform" general ones, and that bag-of-words beats dense models on financial semantic-similarity tasks ([FinMTEB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10990v2)). That study predates voyage-4 and Qwen3, so the gap may be smaller now, and DealPilot should A/B test on its own filings before paying for a finance model.

Retrieval quality depends more on hybrid search and reranking than on the embedding model. pgvector 0.8.7 indexes up to 2,000 dimensions as `vector` and 4,000 as `halfvec`. Storage per row is 4·d+8 bytes for `vector` and 2·d+8 for `halfvec`, and the README recommends fusing Postgres full-text search with vector results through RRF or a cross-encoder ([pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector)). Storing `halfvec(1024)` cuts raw vector storage from about 4.1 GB to 2.06 GB per million chunks (computed from the README formula). Exact tokens such as tickers, line-item labels and covenant terms are where lexical matching beats dense retrieval. For reranking, Voyage lists rerank-2.5-lite at $0.02/M with 200M free tokens, though its doc contradicts itself on whether the free tokens apply ([Voyage](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/pricing)). Local bge-reranker-v2-m3 has no quota at all.

## Long agent jobs rule out most free hosts

A DealPilot job runs 2–15 minutes of LLM calls, EDGAR downloads, PyMuPDF parsing and openpyxl writing. Most free hosting can't run that. **Render's free tier has no background workers, spins down after 15 idle minutes, and its free Postgres expires after 30 days** ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). **Vercel Hobby functions stop at 300 seconds** ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)). Cloudflare Workers Free allows 10 ms of CPU per request ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Fly.io gives new organizations no free tier ([Fly.io](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing)). Railway's post-trial free plan is $1/month of usage ([Railway](https://railway.com/pricing)). Koyeb's free instance is 0.1 vCPU/512 MB and can't run as a worker ([Koyeb](https://www.koyeb.com/docs/reference/instances)). **Hugging Face Docker Spaces now require a paid PRO plan to create** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview)).

Three $0 hosts do fit. **Oracle Always Free gives an always-on Ampere A1 VM with 2 OCPUs and 12 GB RAM** plus 200 GB block storage and 10 TB/month egress ([OCI](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)). That is half the "4 OCPU/24 GB" figure many guides still quote. Oracle can reclaim the instance if its 95th-percentile CPU, network and memory all stay below 20% for 7 days, so an idle demo needs some steady load. **Google Cloud Run** allows 60-minute service requests and **24-hour jobs**, with 180,000 vCPU-seconds and 360,000 GiB-seconds free per month ([Cloud Run](https://cloud.google.com/run/pricing)). The fetched page was truncated, so recheck those numbers; at face value they cover about 300 ten-minute 1 vCPU/2 GiB jobs a month (my calculation). **Modal** gives $30/month of credits ([Modal](https://modal.com/pricing)). At its listed rates, a 10-minute single-core job costs about a cent (my calculation), so the credit covers well over a thousand runs.

Avoid hosted queues and LangGraph's own server. QStash and Inngest only call your HTTP endpoint, so the host's timeout still applies. The free choice is a Postgres-backed queue (procrastinate, pgqueuer, or a `SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` jobs table) on the same database, with `langgraph-checkpoint-postgres` for checkpoints. **Self-hosting LangGraph Agent Server in production needs a license key validated at startup** ([LangChain docs](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/deploy-standalone-server)), and a LangChain forum reply says that is currently Enterprise-only ([forum](https://forum.langchain.com/t/best-practices-for-self-hosting-langgraph-server-oss-without-langgraph-keys/3779)). Running the MIT `langgraph` library inside FastAPI avoids the license.

For Postgres+pgvector, **Neon Free is the best managed option**: 1 GB per project, 100 CU-hours/month, scale-to-zero after 5 minutes, and pgvector on all plans. Its Launch plan bills $0.35/GB-month plus $0.106/CU-hour with no minimum ([Neon](https://neon.com/pricing)). Supabase Free caps the database at 500 MB and **pauses projects after 7 days of inactivity** ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)), which is a problem for a demo that sits idle. A corpus of a few GB at $0 means self-hosting Postgres on Oracle's 200 GB disk. Generated .xlsx and .md files fit in **Cloudflare R2's free 10 GB with zero egress fees** ([R2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)).

For the frontend, **Vercel Hobby is personal and non-commercial only** ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)). A startup demo should use Cloudflare Workers via OpenNext, or Netlify Free (300 credits, with bandwidth at 20 credits/GB) ([Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/)). Keep the dashboard mostly client-rendered so it stays under Workers' CPU limit. data.sec.gov does not support CORS ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)), so all EDGAR calls must go through the FastAPI backend.

For observability, **Langfuse Cloud Hobby gives 50k units/month with 30 days of data access** ([Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/pricing)), and self-hosted Langfuse has been fully MIT-licensed since June 2025 ([Langfuse blog](https://langfuse.com/blog/2025-06-04-open-sourcing-langfuse-product)). Self-hosting needs ClickHouse, Redis and S3, which is too heavy to share a 12 GB VM with the app. **LangSmith Developer gives 5,000 traces/month with 14-day retention** and is the most native fit for LangGraph ([LangChain pricing](https://www.langchain.com/pricing)). One DealPilot run is one LangSmith trace but tens of Langfuse units, so the two free tiers are closer than 5k vs 50k suggests. Helicone reportedly went into maintenance mode after a March 2026 acquisition ([agentmarketcap](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/23/agent-observability-platform-race-wb-weave-arize-phoenix-braintrust-helicone)); avoid it. CI is free on public GitHub repos, and private repos get $12/month of compute, about 2,000 Linux minutes ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions)).

## Fundamentals are free; displayed prices are not

EDGAR is the backbone and costs nothing. It needs no API key, allows **10 requests/second with a declared User-Agent**, updates XBRL APIs within about a minute of a filing, and publishes nightly `companyfacts.zip` and `submissions.zip` bulk files ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data); [SEC APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)). One limitation matters for the forensic agent: **the XBRL APIs return only standard-taxonomy, entity-wide facts, so custom extension tags and segment/dimensional facts are missing**. Those details have to come from the filing text. The undocumented full-text endpoint returned a 10,000-hit cap in a live test ([efts](https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22goodwill%22&forms=10-K)). **edgartools 6.2.0** (MIT, released 15 Nov 2026) builds standardized statements, derives Q4 and TTM figures, extracts 10-K sections and ships an MCP server ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edgartools/)). It replaces most of a home-grown XBRL tag-fallback map.

Valuation inputs are also free with attribution. The Treasury's XML/CSV yield-curve feed needs no key ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/treasury-daily-interest-rate-xml-feed)). FRED requires a key and an exact disclaimer, and **third-party copyrighted series such as ICE BofA spreads need the owner's permission beyond personal use** ([FRED terms](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/terms_of_use.html)). Credit spreads should therefore come from Damodaran's synthetic-rating table. Damodaran's ERP, industry betas and cost-of-capital files were last fully updated **9 January 2026** and are refreshed each January ([Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datacurrent.html)). There is no formal license, so cite them and show only the figures used rather than rehosting the files. SIC codes come free with EDGAR for peer screening. **No free, display-licensed consensus estimates exist**, so projections should be built from historical trends plus guidance extracted from 8-K Item 2.02 exhibits, with each assumption labeled by source.

Market prices are where licensing breaks. yfinance is an unofficial scraper with undisclosed rate limits and reports of IP blocks ([yfinance #2431](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/discussions/2431)), so it is fine for local development only. The official free tiers restrict use. **Tiingo** allows 1,000 requests/day and 500 symbols/month with 30+ years of history, but its internal tiers forbid displaying data to others ([Tiingo](https://www.tiingo.com/about/pricing)). **Twelve Data's** free tier is "internal non-display" only, and **display rights start at Grow, $79/month** ([Twelve Data](https://twelvedata.com/pricing)). Massive (formerly Polygon) free gives 5 calls/minute with plans marked individual use only ([Massive](https://massive.com/pricing)). Alpha Vantage free allows 25 requests/day ([Alpha Vantage](https://www.alphavantage.co/premium/)). EODHD's EOD All-World plan is $19.99/month but is a personal plan ([EODHD](https://eodhd.com/pricing)). The lowest-risk design computes market cap as EDGAR `dei` shares outstanding times one licensed end-of-day close, which keeps everything except the price free. Most commercial display pricing requires contacting sales, so the $79 Twelve Data figure is the cheapest *published* display-rights price found.

## BankerToolBench is real; "FinAgent 133" is not

**BankerToolBench is real and accurately described in the proposal**: 100 end-to-end investment-banking tasks built with 502 bankers, each with a rubric averaging about 150 weighted pass/fail criteria, and Excel, deck and memo deliverables graded inside a data-room environment exposed through MCP servers ([arXiv 2604.11304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11304); [GitHub](https://github.com/Handshake-AI-Research/bankertoolbench)). One correction: grading is done by an **LLM agent verifier (default Gemini 3 Flash Preview) that agrees with experts 88.2% of the time**, against 84.6% agreement between human raters. It is not graded by humans. The code is Apache-2.0 and the data CC-BY-4.0, so a commercial demo is allowed with attribution. Baselines are humbling. **Bankers rated 0% of AI deliverables sendable as-is**, and the best model, GPT-5.4, passed 16% of tasks ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11304)). The authors also found hard-coded spreadsheets where changing the purchase price updated nothing ([the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/500-investment-bankers-review-ai-outputs-and-find-none-ready-for-client-delivery/)). Runs average 700–980 seconds per task with up to 539 LLM calls, and no dollar cost is published. By my estimate a full run costs hundreds of dollars, so use a 10–20 task subset during development.

**No benchmark named "FinAgent Benchmark" with 133 questions and those five categories could be found.** The closest real benchmark is Vals AI's **Finance Agent Benchmark v2.1** (updated 11 Nov 2026): 27 public, 450 licensable private-validation and 449 private test questions, graded by a three-model LLM jury. Claude Fable 5.2 leads at 69.47% ([Vals FAB v2.1](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/fabv2-1)). DealPilot can self-score only on the 27 public questions. The proposal should rename the benchmark or label its 133 questions as an internal set. **FinanceBench** offers 150 open cases with page-level evidence, but under **CC-BY-NC-4.0** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PatronusAI/financebench)), which restricts it to internal use.

The recommended suite has five parts. Use **BTB v1.1** for rubric score and task completion. Use an **internal XBRL gold set** for calculation, temporal and cross-document accuracy, auto-generated from companyfacts (value, unit and accession number as gold, ±0.5% tolerance), plus a spreadsheet input-propagation test that targets the hard-coding failure. Use **FinanceBench-150** to check that citations point to the right page. Use the **Vals FAB v2.1 public set** for research-agent comparability. Use **internal unanswerable-question and labelled risk-flag sets** for hallucination resistance and deal-risk precision/recall. Log cost and latency per task across all of them. The XBRL set costs nothing beyond scripting and a 10% human spot-check, and it is the only part that tests DealPilot's own data pipeline end to end.

## Five levers cut per-task cost by 70–90%

The token budget below is my own estimate; no published DealPilot-like benchmark exists. It assumes ~45–60 calls, **~350K input and ~45K output tokens per company**: research 120K/15K, modeling 60K/5K, forensic 40K/4K, skeptic 30K/5K, verifier 60K/6K, writer 30K/8K, planner 10K/2K. Feeding whole 10-Ks instead of retrieved chunks adds 100–250K input tokens, so **section-targeted RAG is the first lever**. It cuts research input by roughly 60–80% and downstream agents' input by more than 90%.

The second lever is **prompt caching**. Cache reads cost 0.1x on most Claude models and 0.05x on Sonnet/Opus 5.5, and the multipliers stack with the 50% Batch discount ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Gemini caches implicitly on prefixes of 4,096+ tokens ([Gemini caching](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/caching)). DeepSeek cache hits cost 2% of a miss, with another 50% off at off-peak hours ([DeepSeek](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)). Shared system prompts, tool schemas and filing excerpts reused across agent turns should cut effective input cost by 50–85% (estimate). The third lever is **tiered routing**: Haiku 5.5 versus Sonnet 5.5 is a 20x price gap, and gpt-5-nano versus gpt-6.1-sol is 25–40x. Cascades that escalate to the strong model only when the verifier flags a problem put most tokens on the cheap tier. The fourth is **Batch or off-peak pricing** (50% off on Anthropic, OpenAI and Gemini) for overnight screens. The fifth is **strict JSON schemas plus response caching**, which avoid parse-failure retries and repeated peer extractions. DealPilot's deterministic Python valuation is already an optimization in its own right: the expensive models never do arithmetic.

## The recommended stacks and what they cost

The **zero-cost stack** runs on one Oracle A1 VM (2 OCPU/12 GB, 200 GB disk) with Docker Compose: FastAPI, the LangGraph library, a pgqueuer/procrastinate worker, and Postgres+pgvector with `halfvec` and `tsvector` hybrid search. Cloud Run jobs plus Neon Free are the fallback if Oracle has no capacity or reclaims the VM. LLMs go through a LiteLLM router over Gemini free, Groq, Cerebras trial credit, OpenRouter `:free` and local Ollama, with confidential inputs routed only to local models. Search uses Tavily 1k + Exa $10 credit + rate-limited `ddgs`, and extraction uses trafilatura. Embeddings come from Voyage's free tokens or EmbeddingGemma/Qwen3-0.6B, and reranking from local bge-reranker-v2-m3. Data comes from EDGAR via edgartools, Treasury, Damodaran, SIC, and Tiingo free for prices in development only. Storage is R2, the dashboard runs on Cloudflare via OpenNext or Netlify Free, tracing goes to Langfuse Hobby or LangSmith Developer, and CI runs on GitHub Actions. It costs $0 and handles about 1–5 companies a day.

The **low-cost upgrade** keeps the same architecture and swaps in paid parts where the free ones break.

At the standard tier, a 100-company overnight screen costs about $10–30. For comparison, Vals reports leaderboard models at $4–9 *per question* on FAB v2.1 ([Vals](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/fabv2-1)). The gap comes from retrieval discipline and from keeping frontier models out of the bulk of the token flow.

## Conclusion

The cost structure of DealPilot is the reverse of what most people expect. Inference, the part that looks expensive, now costs cents per company once retrieval, caching and an independent cross-family verifier are designed in. Hosting is cheap once long-running jobs leave serverless platforms for a VM or Cloud Run jobs. The fixed costs that remain are licensing, not compute: displayed market prices and, if needed, consensus estimates. The free tiers' training clauses also set a privacy boundary that architecture has to respect. DealPilot's first paid dollar should therefore go to a no-training verifier model, and its second to a price feed with display rights, not to bigger servers.

The benchmark findings change the pitch as much as the budget. BankerToolBench shows frontier agents produce no banker-ready deliverables and often hard-code spreadsheets, so DealPilot's differentiators (deterministic Python valuation, XBRL-grounded citations, skeptic and verifier agents) address the exact failure modes the benchmark documents. Correcting the "FinAgent 133" reference and anchoring claims in BTB plus a reproducible EDGAR gold set makes those claims verifiable. Several figures in this report could not be confirmed on official pages and should be rechecked before committing to a design: free-tier limits that are only visible in vendor consoles, Cloud Run's quota numbers, Oracle's 2 OCPU allowance for older accounts, and every vendor's commercial display pricing.
