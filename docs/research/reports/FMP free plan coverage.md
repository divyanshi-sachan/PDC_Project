# FMP's free plan fails arbitrary tickers; use EDGAR

**Financial Modeling Prep's free "Basic" plan cannot power DealPilot's market-data layer, because it serves a fixed allow-list of about 87 symbols on most endpoints and its terms bar public display of the data.** The plan gives 250 calls per day, 500 MB of trailing-30-day bandwidth and end-of-day data. Quote, statements, ratios, key metrics, market cap and shares-float calls work only for the allow-listed symbols (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA, JPM and the like) and return HTTP 402 for everything else. Our own live probe fits that picture: MSFT, INTC, CSCO, TSLA and JPM pass, while HPQ, DELL, QCOM, ORCL, IBM and HD are rejected, and no natural axis (market cap, exchange, sector, alphabet) explains the split. Only `/profile` accepts any US-traded symbol, and it is the one FMP call worth keeping. The terms separately prohibit showing FMP data on a website or app without a Data Display and Licensing Agreement, even if the page is free, so a public demo is outside the free terms regardless of coverage. Paid tiers start at $29/month ($19 annual) for Starter and rise to $139/month ($99 annual) for Ultimate, but none of them grants display rights; those sit in an Enterprise contract. The cheapest reliable design for DealPilot is to take shares outstanding, sector and peer candidates from SEC EDGAR (already in the pipeline), compute market cap as price times SEC shares inside `packages/finance`, and treat price and beta as the only vendor-licensed inputs, kept behind a swappable adapter. Evidence quality is mixed: no FMP page was readable directly (all returned HTTP 403), so FMP's own statements come from search-result summaries and third-party transcriptions.

## What the free plan provides, and what it does not

The free plan is a taster, not a US-wide data feed. FMP's published limits are 250 requests per day, a 500 MB bandwidth cap over a trailing 30 days, and a 429 response when the quota is exhausted, with the counter resetting the next day ([FMP insights, via search summary](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/insights/platform/can-you-use-fmp-data-in-a-public-app-website-or-client-dashboard)). Third-party transcriptions of the pricing page dated 28 October to 6 November 2026 agree: end-of-day data, profile and reference data, "150+ endpoints", and no free trial ([apis.io](https://apis.io/plans/financial-modeling-prep/financial-modeling-prep-plans-pricing/), [Edgrapi](https://edgrapi.com/blog/financial-modeling-prep-api-pricing), [Narwhal](https://narwhalapi.com/compare/financial-modeling-prep-review)). Statements, key metrics, ratios and several other series are capped at 5 rows per call, and analyst estimates at 10 ([GitHub PR #15](https://github.com/themaainuser/Tradingview-data-grabber/pull/15)). Narwhal and Edgrapi are FMP competitors, so their pricing pages deserve some caution, though their figures match each other and the apis.io transcription.

The more important restriction is on symbols, and the table below combines our probe with the third-party reports. "Probe" means our own call on 2026-11-21 with a free key against `/stable`; "3P" means a third-party source; "DOC" means an FMP page seen only through a search summary.

| Need | Free plan result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

| Market cap endpoint, shares-float, key-metrics-ttm, ratios-ttm, EOD price, income statement | Allow-list only; 402 for HPQ, DELL, QCOM | Probe |

| Peers (`stock-peers`) | FMP FAQ says paid-only; a user-observed 200 on an allow-listed symbol conflicts with that; untested on rejected symbols | DOC via summary ([FAQ](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/faqs)), user observation |

Two practical facts follow from this table. First, the quota is tight: one symbol per call and 5 statement rows per call means roughly 250 quotes a day, or about 80 companies of full statements ([Edgrapi](https://edgrapi.com/blog/financial-modeling-prep-api-pricing)), so everything must be cached. Second, there is no cheap way to find the allow-list. The probe shows that "Restricted Endpoint" means the whole endpoint is off-plan while "Premium Query Parameter" means this symbol is off-plan, which lets code tell the two apart. Unknown tickers return the same symbol-type 402 on `/quote`, so a 402 cannot distinguish a bad ticker from a gated one; `/profile`, which returns 200 with an empty array for unknown symbols, can.

## The allow-list explains why MSFT passes and ORCL fails

The best explanation is a fixed, hand-picked list that is enforced per endpoint, not a rule about the companies themselves. FMP's pricing table, as summarised from its machine-readable page, phrases Basic limits as "Symbol: AAPL, TSLA, AMZN (+84 more)", which is 87 symbols ([FMP pricing-md, via search summary](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/api/pricing-md)). An independent developer probe reports that 39 endpoints accept only a fixed list of 87 stocks and ETFs plus a few indices, FX pairs, crypto and commodities, and that the list matches what the free "All Shares Float" endpoint returns ([GitHub PR #15](https://github.com/themaainuser/Tradingview-data-grabber/pull/15)). Narwhal repeats the same "AAPL, TSLA, AMZN and 84 more" phrasing ([Narwhal](https://narwhalapi.com/compare/financial-modeling-prep-review)). Note that these are not three independent confirmations: the pricing-md text is a search summary, the PR is a single developer's observation, and Narwhal may be reading the same FMP page.

Our own probe supplies the stronger evidence for the pattern. We called `/stable/quote` for 45 tickers on 2026-11-21; the notes record 29 accepted and 16 rejected, though the results table in the notes lists only 43 rows (28 accepted, 15 rejected), so treat the exact counts as approximate. Together with eight previously known accepted symbols (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, NVDA, INTC, CSCO) that gives about 37 accepted tickers, consistent with a list of 87. Every natural hypothesis fails. Market cap fails because the rejected AVGO, ORCL, HD, IBM, CRM and QCOM are larger than the accepted F, GM, ROKU, ETSY, SNAP and SHOP. Exchange fails because ADRs TSM, BABA, SONY and SHOP pass while NVO fails, and NYSE and NASDAQ names sit on both sides. Sector fails because technology splits (AMD and ADBE pass; AVGO, TXN, MU, ORCL, CRM and IBM fail) and so do industrials (BA and GE pass; CAT fails). Alphabetical order fails too. The pattern looks like a "popular tickers" list, since it mixes mega caps with retail favourites such as F, GM, ROKU and SNAP, but that origin is an inference; we found no forum post, FMP page or other source explaining the membership. FMP has an article titled "Why an FMP Endpoint May Work for One Symbol but Not Another" that we could not read, and it may contain the official answer ([FMP insights](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/insights/platform/why-an-fmp-endpoint-may-work-for-one-symbol-but-not-another)).

We rate confidence as moderate-high that a fixed list exists and low on its exact membership. The full list was not found, the date the rule began is unknown, and BRK-B was rejected in our probe while a separate report shows BRK.B also returning 402 on `/profile`, so share-class tickers may be a special case. The one clear policy change in the record is that the legacy `/api/v3` endpoints return 403 for keys created after 2025-08-31, so code must use `/stable` ([claude-trading-skills PR #1](https://github.com/fabbo-repo/claude-trading-skills/pull/1)). For rejected symbols such as HPQ, DELL and QCOM, `/profile` returned 200 while market-capitalization, shares-float, key-metrics-ttm, ratios-ttm, EOD price and income-statement all returned 402 (our probe). That makes `/profile` the only free route to beta and sector for arbitrary US tickers, with the caveat that we have not yet inspected its fields. If it carries price and market cap, shares outstanding can be approximated as market cap divided by price, an unverified approximation that DealPilot should not publish under its provenance rules.

## Public display is outside the free terms

FMP's Terms of Service, section 2.2.2 as summarised by search, prohibit showcasing FMP data on websites, blogs, software products or applications without a specific agreement ([FMP Terms of Service, via search summary](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/terms-of-service)). FMP's guidance says a free public page can still need a display agreement because the audience sees the data regardless of revenue, that paying for an individual plan does not change this, and that attribution is not a substitute for permission; it also calls the free plan an evaluation plan even for commercial use cases ([FMP insights, via search summary](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/insights/platform/can-you-use-fmp-data-in-a-public-app-website-or-client-dashboard)). The commercial pricing page says display or redistribution requires a Data Display and Licensing Agreement ([FMP pricing](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/pricing-plans?planType=commercial)). Enterprise carries those rights with no published price ([Edgrapi](https://edgrapi.com/blog/financial-modeling-prep-api-pricing)). Personal plans cover one individual's non-business use, and a multi-user site needs a separate agreement ([Narwhal](https://narwhalapi.com/compare/financial-modeling-prep-review)).

The consequence is direct. A public DealPilot URL that renders live FMP numbers breaches the terms as published, on any tier below Enterprise. A private or local demo seen only by the owner arguably falls within personal use. We found no evidence of a non-commercial portfolio exception, and the only way to learn whether FMP would grant one is to ask them. The wording above rests on summaries, not on the pages themselves, so confirm it against the live Terms before relying on it either way.

## Paid tiers add coverage, not display rights

Pricing as transcribed from the FMP page on 2026-10-28 and matched by Edgrapi on 2026-11-06: **Starter $29/month ($19 annual)** with US coverage, 300 calls/minute, 5 years of history and annual fundamentals; **Premium $69/month ($49 annual)** with 750 calls/minute, 30 years, and UK and Canada added; **Ultimate $139/month ($99 annual)** with 3,000 calls/minute, global coverage, transcripts, 13F and bulk files; Enterprise custom ([apis.io](https://apis.io/plans/financial-modeling-prep/financial-modeling-prep-plans-pricing/), [Edgrapi](https://edgrapi.com/blog/financial-modeling-prep-api-pricing)). One older figure set of $22, $59 and $149 appears elsewhere and is likely stale ([Qveris](https://qveris.ai/guides/financial-modeling-prep-api)). Starter should remove the 87-symbol limit for US companies, but that is inferred from plan descriptions. It is unconfirmed whether `stock-peers` and the screener open on Starter, and Edgrapi flags several datasets as "Limited" even on Premium. Because Starter is still a personal-use plan, paying $29 would fix coverage for private use but not the public-display problem.

## Recommended architecture: own the shares, rent only the price

The design below keeps vendor-licensed data to a single number per ticker and leaves everything else on public, provenance-friendly sources.

**Shares outstanding, sector and peer candidates come from EDGAR.** Shares are the cover-page `dei` value already reachable through `services/edgar`, which satisfies the repo rule that every fact carries accession number, period and unit. Sector and industry come from the SIC code, and peer candidates come from SIC groupings, refined by the existing research agent's extraction of competitor lists from filings, with Exa's similar-company search as an optional aid ([the earlier cost-stack report](DealPilot%20low%20cost%20stack.md) covers both). This removes FMP's `stock-peers` and `shares-float` dependence, which fail on most tickers anyway.

**Market cap is computed, never fetched.** Define it as one end-of-day close times SEC shares in `packages/finance`, with the price date and the filing's cover-page date both stored, because the two almost never coincide and the gap must be visible to the user. This also satisfies the non-negotiable that no number is LLM-generated.

**Beta has two options, ordered by licensing risk.** The lowest-risk choice is Damodaran's industry betas, which are free, updated each January (last on 9 January 2026) and used with citation and only the figures needed ([Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datacurrent.html)); they are industry betas rather than company betas, which is acceptable for a CAPM discount rate and should be labelled as such. A company-specific beta can instead be regressed in `packages/finance` from a price history, which needs only an end-of-day price series and keeps the formula deterministic and testable. Vendor betas from Finnhub or FMP `/profile` are the third choice, for private use only.

**Price sits behind one adapter, with different providers for different audiences.** For local and owner-only use, take the price from FMP `/profile` (any US ticker, one call, but verify that the field exists), falling back to Finnhub or Twelve Data Basic. All of this is within personal use and costs nothing. For the public web demo, there are three honest paths. The first is to let the user enter or confirm the price, with an as-of date, and show no vendor number at all. The second is to show only pre-run, cached, clearly dated examples and obtain written permission from the vendor, which costs nothing but may be refused. The third is to pay for display rights: Twelve Data Grow at $79/month is the cheapest published price found, whereas FMP's route is an Enterprise quote with no published price. Given the portfolio context and the project's credit discipline, the first path is the recommended default.

**Operational rules.** Cache every vendor response in Neon Postgres with TTLs (end-of-day prices need no more than one call per ticker per day), treat 402 "Premium Query Parameter" as "symbol not entitled" and fall through to the next adapter, and never probe FMP to discover its allow-list because the 250-call quota makes that expensive. Keep each provider in a separate adapter in `packages/sources` so a tier change swaps one module. The Verifier should recompute market cap from the cached price and SEC shares and flag any figure whose price date is stale.

## Conclusion

The headline lesson is that FMP's free key is an evaluation key with a quietly enforced shortlist, not a smaller version of a US-wide product, and that the shortlist is invisible until a call fails. That reframes the build decision: for a portfolio project the scarce resource is not API calls but display rights, and no free tier offers them. Designing around that constraint, by deriving market cap, shares, sector and peers from EDGAR and shrinking the licensed input to one dated closing price, turns a licensing problem into an ordinary data-provenance feature the Verifier can audit.

Several items remain open and are cheap to resolve. Inspect the fields returned by `/profile` for HPQ, DELL and QCOM to confirm price, market cap, beta and sector are present. Test Finnhub's `profile2`, `peers` and `metric` on a rejected ticker with a free key. Read the FMP Terms and the "Why an FMP Endpoint May Work for One Symbol but Not Another" article in a browser, since every FMP page returned HTTP 403 to our fetch tool. And reconcile the probe count (45 tickers reported, 43 rows tabulated) before the results are cited elsewhere.
